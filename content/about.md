Barefeed publishes research syntheses on three subjects: **logistics**, **finance**, and
**learning**. The output is not news and it is not opinion — it is existing research,
industry data, and primary sources read carefully and rewritten into something a busy
professional can actually use.

## What a synthesis is

Most writing about these topics is either a press release restated at length or an
author's intuition dressed up as analysis. A synthesis sits in between: it takes named
primary sources, checks them against each other, notes where they disagree, and
presents the conclusion with its confidence level attached.

When the evidence supports a clear conclusion, the article says so. When the evidence is
thin or contested, the article says that too. **Claiming more confidence than the
sources warrant is the failure mode this publication is built to avoid.**

## Who writes it

Barefeed is a single publication, not a newsroom. Content is produced with AI
assistance and reviewed by a human editor before publication. The byline is
deliberately the publication rather than a person or an avatar — there is no implied
team, and no implied first-hand experience the articles do not have.

Every article traces its claims to named sources, cited inline, so any reader can check
the work.

## What is not covered

- **Breaking news.** Barefeed has no reporting desk and no claim to timeliness.
- **Investment advice.** Finance articles explain valuation *method*. They are not
  recommendations, and nothing here should be read as a buy or sell signal.
- **Sponsored content.** There is none, and there will not be without a prominent
  disclosure at the top of the article.

## Corrections

Errors are corrected in place, with the change noted at the bottom of the article rather
than silently patched. If something is wrong, it stays visible that it was wrong and
when.
